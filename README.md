# Honeycomb Bakery | Third-Party Risk Management

**End-to-End Supplier Risk Assessment, Due Diligence, Risk Treatment and Executive Assurance**

**Project Type:** GRC Portfolio Case Study  
**Industry:** Retail, Food & Beverage, E-commerce  
**Geographic Scope:** United Kingdom and United States  
**Assessment Date:** 14 November 2026  
**Status:** Simulated assessment, proposed management decision pending approval

Executive Summary
This project demonstrates a structured, risk-based approach to Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM), using Honeycomb Bakery, a fictional multinational retail and food services organisation.
The assessment focuses on a small delivery partner that processes customer information and supports a material proportion of Honeycomb's online order fulfilment.
The supplier was already operational before a formal risk assessment was completed, creating potential exposure relating to information security, data protection, service continuity, contractual safeguards and supplier oversight.
The project applies a proportionate supplier assurance methodology to identify inherent risks, assess business criticality, document evidence requirements, develop remediation actions and present a defensible management decision.
The central challenge is determining whether the organisation should continue using a business-critical supplier when sufficient evidence of its control effectiveness has not yet been obtained.

## 1. Business Context

Honeycomb Bakery operates:

- 12 retail stores, comprising eight in the UK and four in the US.
- An online ordering platform supporting customer purchases and delivery fulfilment.
- Approximately 200 employees.
- Third-party relationships supporting business operations and customer services.

The delivery partner receives customer names, delivery addresses, telephone numbers and order contents through its own driver application and infrastructure.

Because the supplier handles a material share of online fulfilment, a security incident or prolonged service interruption could affect customer privacy, operational continuity, revenue and brand reputation.

The supplier's relatively small size does not automatically make it low risk. The assessment considers the sensitivity of information processed, service dependency, potential business impact and available control assurance.

## 2. Assessment Objectives

The project was designed to demonstrate the ability to:

1. Establish a risk-based supplier assessment scope and methodology.
2. Distinguish inherent risk from business criticality.
3. Determine proportionate due diligence requirements.
4. Identify evidence gaps without assuming that missing documentation proves a control failure.
5. Assess relevant contractual, security and privacy safeguards.
6. Develop risk treatment and remediation recommendations.
7. Define ongoing supplier monitoring and escalation requirements.
8. Establish an exit and offboarding approach.
9. Present risk information and recommendations to an accountable executive risk owner.

## 3. Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows the third-party lifecycle:

**Supplier identification → Inherent risk assessment → Criticality assessment → Due diligence → Control evaluation → Risk treatment → Management decision → Continuous monitoring → Offboarding**

### Inherent Risk Assessment

Inherent risk considers the potential exposure associated with the supplier relationship before relying on the effectiveness of mitigating controls.

The scenario's illustrative inherent cyber/privacy risk score is **16/25, High**.

### Business Criticality

Criticality is assessed separately from inherent risk. It considers the business consequences of supplier failure, including interruption to online fulfilment and customer service.

The delivery partner is assessed as **Critical** because of Honeycomb's operational dependency.

### Control Effectiveness and Residual Risk

The assessment does not assume that a supplier control is effective simply because a policy, certification or assurance document has been requested.

At the current assessment stage:

- Control effectiveness has not been independently verified.
- Requested evidence remains outstanding.
- Residual risk has not been assigned a numerical score.
- Formal risk acceptance has not been recorded.

This approach avoids creating unsupported assurance conclusions.

## 5. Key Risk Themes

### Information Security and Customer Data

The supplier processes customer information outside Honeycomb's direct infrastructure.

The assessment considers whether access restrictions, authentication, data handling, incident response and other appropriate safeguards can be demonstrated.

### Operational Resilience

A material dependency on the delivery partner creates potential exposure to service disruption.

The assessment considers business continuity arrangements, incident escalation, recovery expectations and alternative fulfilment arrangements.

### Contractual and Regulatory Risk

The assessment identifies the need to validate contractual security obligations, data protection responsibilities, incident notification requirements and relevant supplier oversight provisions.

### Third-Party Governance

The absence of a completed pre-contract assessment raises concerns about the supplier onboarding and risk approval process.

The assessment therefore considers governance responsibilities, due diligence requirements, approval authority and ongoing monitoring.

## 6. Management Decision and Risk Treatment

The proposed decision is a **30-day conditional continuation**, subject to approval by the appropriate authorised risk owner.

This is not a confirmed approval or completed risk acceptance.

Before a defensible final decision can be made, the organisation would need to:

- Obtain and evaluate relevant supplier evidence.
- Confirm applicable contractual and data protection obligations.
- Validate material security and operational controls.
- Address significant gaps through accountable remediation actions.
- Assess whether compensating safeguards are required.
- Determine residual risk based on supported control effectiveness.
- Record the decision, approval authority, conditions and review date.

The recommendation recognises the need to balance operational continuity with appropriate risk management.

## 7. Ongoing Monitoring and Supplier Exit

The proposed monitoring approach includes risk-based supplier reassessment, tracking of remediation commitments, review of security and operational incidents, and escalation of material changes.

The offboarding approach considers access revocation, return or deletion of customer information, contractual obligations, transition planning and verification of completed exit activities.

Together, these demonstrate that TPRM extends beyond initial supplier onboarding.

## 10. How I Would Progress This Assessment in Practice

The next stage of a real engagement would involve:

1. Issuing the formal supplier evidence request.
2. Interviewing relevant supplier security and operations personnel.
3. Confirming processing roles, data flows and contractual requirements.
4. Evaluating actual security and operational evidence.
5. Testing relevant integration and access controls.
6. Performing a business continuity walkthrough.
7. Updating the risk assessment based on validated findings.
8. Determining residual risk and treatment requirements.
9. Obtaining documented management approval.
10. Establishing ongoing monitoring and evidence-based closure criteria.

## 11. Portfolio Disclosure and Evidence Integrity

**This is a fictional, educational case study, not a real supplier audit or client engagement.**

Honeycomb Bakery, the delivery partner, management roles, dates, evidence references, findings and proposed decisions are simulated.

No actual vendor interview, technical security test, contract examination or independent assurance engagement was conducted.

Evidence identifiers beginning with `REQ` represent requested documentation, not received evidence. An evidence gap does not automatically establish that the underlying security control is absent.

Any future sample supplier responses must be clearly identified as **SYNTHETIC TRAINING EVIDENCE** and assessed separately for sufficiency.

No real customer information, confidential supplier records or proprietary audit evidence should be added to this public repository.
