# Five priority contract protections

**Status:** proposed contract addendum; actual signed terms not available for review. Legal counsel must adapt language to governing law and the parties' actual controller/processor roles.

1. **Breach notification (must have):** Supplier notifies Honeycomb without undue delay and within 24 hours of becoming aware of a suspected or confirmed personal-data breach, sharing available facts, categories, scope, containment and regular updates. **Risk:** delayed containment and missed reporting obligations. The 24-hour term is a proposed contractual requirement, not the statutory deadline.
2. **Security and permitted processing (must have):** Process only on documented instructions; maintain role-based access, MFA for privileged users, encryption in transit and at rest, appropriate logging and secure driver deprovisioning. **Risk:** unauthorised access and data misuse.
3. **Sub-processors and transfers (must have):** Prior specific or general written authorisation, current sub-processor list, notice of changes, flow-down protections and lawful transfer mechanisms where required. **Risk:** unapproved onward processing and cross-border exposure.
4. **Continuity and service levels (must have):** Defined delivery SLAs, outage escalation, recovery objectives, contingency cooperation, testing and transition support. **Risk:** disruption of material online revenue.
5. **Exit, return and verified deletion (must have):** Return data in usable format; revoke all credentials; delete or securely isolate personal data within 30 days except lawful retention; provide sub-processor confirmations and signed destruction evidence. **Risk:** lock-in, orphaned access and residual personal data.

**Additional legal check:** confirm Article 28 UK GDPR terms where the partner acts as processor, audit/information rights, assistance with data subject requests, incident cooperation, international transfer mechanisms, liability and governing law. Do not assume UK GDPR covers every US order; assess relevant jurisdiction and data flows.
