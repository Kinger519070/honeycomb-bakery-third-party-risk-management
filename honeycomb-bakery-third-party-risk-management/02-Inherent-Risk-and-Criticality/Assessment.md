# Inherent risk and criticality

## Method
Illustrative 5×5 risk matrix: likelihood 1 (rare) to 5 (almost certain); impact 1 (insignificant) to 5 (severe). Score = likelihood × impact. 1-4 Low; 5-9 Moderate; 10-15 High; 16-25 Very High for numeric scoring. The qualitative **inherent vendor tier** is High, using data sensitivity, external app/infrastructure, personal-data exposure and integration. Numeric 16/25 is a scenario-based example, **not a statistically estimated probability**.

## Inherent risk: High
The partner holds customer names, home addresses, phone numbers and order contents on its own systems. Possible harms include unauthorised driver access, app/API compromise, unlawful onward disclosure, excessive retention and incident-response delays. It does not directly process payment card data. Small company size does not prove weak controls; control effectiveness is unverified.

Illustrative inherent scenario: likelihood 4 × impact 4 = **16/25**, above Honeycomb's 12-point treatment threshold. Validate after vendor engagement.

## Criticality: Critical
The partner is materially responsible for online fulfilment. Loss of service could cause immediate unfulfilled orders, refunds, complaints, reputation harm and revenue interruption. Recovery time, substitutability and alternate capacity are unknown. Criticality reflects Honeycomb's dependency, not vendor security maturity or spend.

## Why they diverge
The supplier can have a High inherent cyber/privacy exposure while being Critical to operations. Criticality worries me more because a prolonged outage could immediately interrupt a material revenue channel even if information-security controls are effective. Mitigate both, with an urgent continuity alternative.

## Residual risk
**Not yet rateable.** No control evidence has been supplied. Do not subtract hypothetical mitigations or imply the vendor has passed assurance.
